Solar & Battery Bank Engineering Methodology

Undersizing an off-grid or hybrid solar power system results in chronic battery sulfation, inverter overload trips during compressor startup, and unexpected grid dependency during cloudy weather. Conversely, oversizing needlessly inflates upfront capital expenditure (CapEx). Calcvora applies IEEE and NEC-aligned photovoltaic sizing equations to balance generation, storage, and surge capacity.

1. Daily Energy Audit (Watt-Hours)

Every appliance's continuous power draw (Watts) is multiplied by its quantity and actual compressor/active duty hours per day:

Daily Energy Demand (Wh/day) = Σ (Appliance_Watts × Quantity × Run_Hours)

2. Pure Sine Wave Inverter & Surge Sizing

Inductive motor loads—such as air conditioner compressors, refrigerators, and water pumps—draw 2.5× to 4× their rated running wattage for 1–3 seconds upon startup. Furthermore, inverters are rated in apparent power (VA) at a typical 0.80 power factor:

Minimum Inverter Rating (VA) = (Total_Connected_Watts × 1.25 Safety Margin) ÷ 0.80 Power Factor

3. Battery Bank Storage & Depth of Discharge (DoD)

Battery nameplate capacity is never 100% usable. Discharging a Lead-Acid battery beyond 50% drastically shortens cycle life, whereas Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) cells comfortably sustain 80% Depth of Discharge over 4,000–6,000 cycles. We also account for DC-to-AC inverter conversion losses:

Required Battery Bank (Wh) = (Daily_Energy_Wh × Days_of_Autonomy) ÷ (Max_DoD × Inverter_Efficiency)
Nominal Amp-Hours (Ah) = Required_Battery_Wh ÷ System_DC_Voltage

4. Solar PV Array & MPPT Charge Controller Sizing

Standard Test Conditions (STC) rate solar panels at 25°C cell temperature and 1,000 W/m² irradiance. Under real rooftop conditions, thermal heat buildup, dust soiling, wiring resistance, and battery charging round-trip losses reduce net yield by ~23% (a 0.77 derating factor):

Required Solar Array (W) = Daily_Energy_Wh ÷ (Peak_Sun_Hours × Derating_Factor × Battery_Efficiency)
Minimum MPPT Controller Current (A) = (Required_Solar_Watts ÷ System_DC_Voltage) × 1.25

Worked Numerical Example: 1-Bedroom Home with Inverter AC

Consider a household running a 1.5-Ton Inverter AC (1,400W × 5 hrs = 7,000 Wh), an Inverter Refrigerator (150W × 12 hrs = 1,800 Wh), 3 Ceiling Fans (225W × 10 hrs = 2,250 Wh), and LED Lights + Router (100W × 8 hrs = 800 Wh):

  1. Total Daily Load: 7,000 + 1,800 + 2,250 + 800 = 11,850 Wh (11.85 kWh/day).
  2. Battery Bank (48V LiFePO4, 1 Day Autonomy): 11,850 ÷ (0.80 × 0.92) = 16,100 Wh (16.1 kWh), or 335 Ah at 48V.
  3. Solar Array (5.0 Peak Sun Hours): 11,850 ÷ (5.0 × 0.77 × 0.95) = 3,240 W, satisfied by six 550W monocrystalline panels (3,300W).
  4. MPPT Controller: (3,300W ÷ 48V) × 1.25 = 85.9A (select a 90A or 100A 48V MPPT charge controller).

System Optimization Tips

  • Upgrade to 48V Above 3 kW: Moving from 24V to 48V halves DC bus current, reducing expensive copper battery cable gauge requirements and minimizing I²R resistive losses.
  • Shift Heavy Loads to Solar Noon: Running water pumps, washing machines, and daytime AC between 10:00 AM and 3:00 PM feeds power directly from solar panels to loads, bypassing battery charge/discharge wear.
  • Use Series-Parallel String Matching: Ensure your solar panel string open-circuit voltage (Voc) stays at least 15% below your MPPT controller's maximum input voltage rating on the coldest winter morning.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many solar panels and batteries do I need to run a 1.5 ton inverter AC?

A 1.5-ton inverter air conditioner averages roughly 1,200W to 1,500W once the room reaches target temperature. Running it for 8 hours daily consumes ~11.2 kWh. With 5 Peak Sun Hours and 0.77 system derating, you need roughly 3,000W to 3,200W of solar panels (six 550W panels) and a 15 kWh (48V 300Ah) LiFePO4 battery bank for overnight backup.

Why is LiFePO4 battery capacity sized smaller than Lead-Acid for the same backup?

Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) batteries safely support an 80% to 90% Depth of Discharge (DoD) and ~95% round-trip efficiency. Tubular or AGM Lead-Acid batteries degrade rapidly if discharged below 50% DoD and lose ~18% of energy as heat during charging, requiring nearly 1.8x more nameplate Amp-hours for identical usable energy.

Should I choose a 12V, 24V, or 48V battery bank?

Use 12V for small loads under 1,500W (vans, cabins), 24V for medium loads up to 3,500W, and 48V for whole-home or air-conditioning loads above 3,500W. Higher DC voltage cuts current (Amps) by up to 4x, reducing copper cable thickness, resistive heat losses, and MPPT controller costs.

What is the solar derating factor (0.77) in sizing equations?

A 550W solar panel rarely outputs 550W in real-world conditions due to high cell temperature losses (~10%), dust and soiling (~5%), DC/AC wiring voltage drop (~3%), and module mismatch. Multiplying Peak Sun Hours by a 0.77 derating factor ensures your array meets daily energy demand year-round.

How is MPPT solar charge controller amperage calculated?

Divide the total solar array wattage by the nominal battery bank voltage, then multiply by a 1.25 NEC safety factor to account for cold-weather cloud-edge irradiance spikes. For example, a 3,300W array on a 48V bank requires (3300 / 48) × 1.25 = 85.9A, meaning a 90A or 100A MPPT controller is required.
